  deploy: `0103` (purchase orders in the inbox), `0104` (Company Settings),
  `0105` (statement balances) and `0106` (estimates), after Riham's `0102`

  Before using the deployed site, check the deploy finished: Setup → Company
  Settings should open.

  `npm -w @accounting/shared run build` once (the shared package gained the
  settings, the bank-account rule and the estimate forms). `npm run db:seed`
  adds five demo estimates to each company (it skips what is already there).
  The deployed site has no demo estimates: make one in step 20.
- Sign in as a **firm admin**: Company Settings can only be changed by one, and
  the firm view (step 2) is theirs. Locally that is `admin@example.com`.

# Part 2: A customer's purchase order becomes an invoice

Item 23 from 10-05: upload what a customer sends to order from the client, and
get the sales invoice from it.

- [ ] **5. Upload it.** AI → Document Inbox. Drop
  `samples/2026-10-20-customer-purchase-order.pdf` on the upload box (or choose
  it). After a few seconds the line under the box says it was read as a
  "customer purchase order". It also works by email, like any other document.
- [ ] **6. It waits under Invoices.** Open the Invoices tab. The order is there
  with a blue **PO** tag, Harbor View Cafe, #PO-7781, 1,122.00. The new

  "PO — Purchase order from Customer", and the lines keep their quantities
  (2 grinders at 349.00, 12 pitchers at 14.50, 1 training at 250.00).
- [ ] **7. Make the invoice.** Pick the customer (or Add new customer: Harbor
  View Cafe). Invoice date is today; change it if the goods ship later. Choose
  the revenue account for each line. Click **Create sales invoice**.
- [ ] **8. Check it.** Open History, then the APPROVED link, or go to Invoices.
  The new invoice has the next invoice number (not PO-7781: that is the
  customer's number), the memo "Customer PO #PO-7781", the customer's terms, and
  the three lines with their quantities and unit prices. It is posted, so
  Accounts Receivable is 1,122.00 higher.

# Part 3: Company Settings

New: Setup → **Company Settings**, each client's own defaults, like QuickBooks'
Account and settings → Sales and Expenses. Before November 1, each real client
needs its own invoice numbering and terms.

- [ ] **9. The page.** Setup → Company Settings. Two cards, Sales and Expenses.
  Under the invoice number boxes it says what the next invoice will be. Only a
  firm admin can save; an accountant sees it read-only.
- [ ] **10. Invoice numbers.** Set the prefix to `INV-` and Start at to `5000`,
  then Save. The line now reads "The next invoice will be INV-5000". + New →
  Invoice: the number box says INV-5000. Recurring invoices, sales orders and
  purchase orders (step 8) are numbered the same way now; before, they came out
  as INV-0001 beside the form's 1001. A corrected invoice (INV-1042-R1) no longer
  throws the next number off. Clear the prefix and Start at again afterwards if
  this is a real company.
- [ ] **11. Default terms.** Set "Default terms for invoices" to Net 15 and
  Save. + New → Invoice: Terms starts at Net 15 and the due date is 15 days out;
  picking a customer still uses that customer's own terms. Add a new customer:
  it gets Net 15. "Default terms for bills" does the same for + New → Bill and
  new vendors.
- [ ] **12. Default sales tax.** Pick a tax code under "Default sales tax"
  (Setup → Tax Codes has them) and Save. + New → Invoice: the tax box starts on
  it.
- [ ] **13. Default payment method.** Set it to Check and Save. + New →
  Expense: Payment method starts on Check. Save and new keeps Check.

# Part 4: Security and backups

Item 28 (security review, recovery plan) and item 43 (backups). Before real
client data arrives on November 1. Little of it shows on screen; the write-ups
are [`../qa/2026-10-20-security-review.md`](../qa/2026-10-20-security-review.md),
[`../guides/recovery-plan.md`](../guides/recovery-plan.md) and
[`../guides/backups.md`](../guides/backups.md).

- [ ] **14. One client's records stay with that client.** To tell: every
  change in the app now looks the record up within the client in the address,
  so a record's id from another client is "not found". Before, someone limited
  to one client could post, void or change another client's invoice, period,
  vendor or pay run by its id. Ids are long and random, so it needed an id they
  had no way to see, but a client login must not have that gap. Statements and
  invoices in the AI inbox, and the payee on checks and expenses, had the same
  gap and are fixed too.
- [ ] **15. Sign-in.** To show: sign out, and try a wrong password five times on
  a test login: it is held for 15 minutes (this was already so). New: 20 wrong
  sign-ins from one address in 15 minutes refuse that address, whichever logins
  it tries ("Too many sign-in attempts from this network"). And a wrong email
  now takes as long to answer as a wrong password, so timing no longer tells
  which emails have logins. After this deploy, Setup → Activity Log should show
  each person's own address, not Railway's.
- [ ] **16. Files and the browser.** To tell: an attachment of a type that could
  run code (a web page, an SVG) is only ever downloaded, never shown in the app,
  and the web app now tells the browser not to load scripts from anywhere else.
  That last part only reports for now (browser console, F12); it is switched on
  once the live site shows no reports.
- [ ] **17. Backups and the recovery plan.** To tell: the backup tool and its
  restore check are built; the first run against production needs the
  connection string from Railway. The recovery plan covers the app down, a bad
  change, the database or files lost, a leaked secret and a break-in. The one
  thing to act on now: keep `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEY` in a password manager
  outside Railway. Without it a restored database cannot read SSNs, tax IDs or
  two-step sign-in secrets.

# Part 5: Statement balances

Item 72, the half that is Aheed's: reconciling should take the statement's
ending balance and date from a statement already uploaded. Statements now keep
them. Filling them in on the reconciliation page is Riham's half, still to do.
Use a bank statement with the account number masked to the last four digits.

- [ ] **18. Read and checked.** AI → Document Inbox, upload a bank statement.
  Open it under Statements. Under "Bank account (this statement)" is a new row:
  Period from, Period to, Beginning balance and Ending balance, read off the
  statement. Below them: "The lines add up" when the beginning balance plus the
  lines comes to the ending balance. Change the ending balance by 100: it now
  says the lines are 100.00 off, which is how a line the AI missed shows before
  anything is posted. Put it back. On a card statement the boxes read Previous
  balance, New balance and Closing date.
- [ ] **19. Kept for Reconcile.** Pick the bank account and approve. The
  statement now keeps its period, its balances and the account it was approved
  against (before, only the account name printed on it, often blank). This is
  what Riham's reconciliation page will fill its ending balance and date from.
  Statements approved before today have no account and are not used.

# Part 6: Estimates

Item 17: Estimate was the last transaction type the QA audit found missing. A
quote to a customer that becomes an invoice when the work goes ahead. Nothing
about it is in the books until then.

- [ ] **20. Make one.** + New → **Estimate** (or Accounts Receivable →
  Estimates → New estimate, or New estimate on a customer's page). Pick a
  customer, set an expiration date, add two lines with a quantity and rate, pick
  a sales tax. The total adds up as you type. Save: it is EST-0001 (each
  company counts its own), Pending.
- [ ] **21. Accept or reject it.** On the estimate, **Mark accepted** asks who
  accepted it and when; the status turns Accepted. **Set back to pending**
  undoes it. **Mark rejected** turns it Rejected, and then it cannot be invoiced
  until it is set back. A pending estimate past its expiration date reads
  **Expired** in the list (the demo data has one); it can still be accepted or
  invoiced. ⋯ → Save as PDF prints it headed ESTIMATE, with the expiration date
  and the total.
- [ ] **22. Make the invoice.** **Create invoice** (on the estimate, or in the
  list's Action column). The new invoice opens: the next invoice number from
  Company Settings, today's date, the customer's terms, the estimate's lines,
  quantities and message, and "From estimate EST-0001". It is a draft:
  **Post invoice** puts it in the books (an accountant's step, as for any
  invoice staff enter).
- [ ] **23. Closed.** Back on the estimate: Closed, with the invoice it became.
  It can no longer be edited, invoiced again or deleted. The top-bar search
  finds it by number or customer, and the customer's page lists it.
